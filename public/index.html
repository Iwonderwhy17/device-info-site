A browser is more than a window onto the web. It sends technical details with requests, stores site data, and exposes a collection of capabilities that websites can sometimes combine into a fingerprint.

01 / The quiet metadata leak

Some information is needed for ordinary web functionality. A site may need to know whether a screen is narrow, which language to display, or whether a feature is supported. The privacy question is not simply whether a value exists. It is whether a site needs it, whether it is shared, and whether several values can be combined to distinguish one browser from another.

This page reads a limited set of browser properties locally. The values below are displayed in your browser and are not sent to this server.

02 / Cookies: useful, occasionally nosy

Cookies are small pieces of text associated with a website. They can keep a session signed in or remember a preference. They can also be used for tracking when sites and embedded services share identifiers. This demo creates one harmless first-party cookie named privacy_lab_demo. It contains only a short demo value and is never sent to another service.

03 / Fingerprinting, with the brakes on

Fingerprinting combines browser and device characteristics. A canvas rendering result, WebGL renderer details, and font measurements can add entropy. These checks are imperfect: results vary by browser, operating system, graphics driver, privacy settings, and software updates.

The checks below run only after you press the button. They run locally and do not upload their results.

06 / What this lab deliberately does not do

This page does not exploit browser vulnerabilities, silently enumerate your device, probe your local network, attempt WebRTC address discovery, inspect other tabs, or read browsing history.

For a meaningful privacy review, keep your browser updated, restrict third-party cookies, review extensions, use HTTPS, and avoid installing extensions you do not trust. Privacy settings reduce exposure; they do not make a browser invisible.
